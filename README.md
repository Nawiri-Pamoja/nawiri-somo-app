# Nawiri Pamoja – Dholuo ↔ English learning app

**Use it now: [somo.nawiri-pamoja.org](https://somo.nawiri-pamoja.org)** (open once with internet, install, then it works offline)

A free, offline learning app for Luo children in rural Kenya, made by the grassroots NGO
[Nawiri Pamoja](https://nawiri-pamoja.org). It takes a child who speaks only Dholuo from the very first sounds
to **reading and writing**, then to everyday English, whole sentences and the English needed to use a computer
and the internet. It works the other way round too: English speakers can use it to learn Dholuo.

It runs in the browser of an Android tablet or phone, is installed once, and then works **without internet**.

## Why it is built this way

- **Many children have nothing else.** For some, this tablet may be the only teacher of reading and writing they get.
  So the course is complete, patient and step by step, and it never punishes mistakes.
- **Children learn without an adult.** Screens use pictures, sound and big buttons, with very little text.
  A lesson takes about five minutes.
- **Reading first in the mother tongue.** Dholuo is written almost exactly as it is spoken. Children learn the letters
  with words they already know (*mama, ot, rech, guok*), and then transfer reading to English.
- **What you read is what you write.** The app has its own school-print font. Its letters are drawn from the same
  strokes the children trace, so the *a* on the screen is the round *a* with a stick that children write by hand.
- **One tablet, many children.** Picture profiles instead of passwords, a two-player game on one screen,
  and a shared village score (“Pamoja”) instead of a ranking.
- **The community owns the content.** Native speakers check, correct, extend and record everything inside the app.

## The learning path (9 stages, about 255 lessons)

| Stage | What children do | Lessons |
|---|---|---|
| A · *Winji* – Listen | Pictures and sound only, no reading: animals, food, body, family, numbers, colours | 14 |
| B · *Som gi ndik* – Read and write | The full Dholuo literacy course with 32 reading booklets (see below) | 80 |
| B2 · English letters | English letter sounds, writing A–Z, first English words, sight words | 22 |
| C · *Weche mokwongo* – First words | Core vocabulary, greetings, first sentence patterns | 21 |
| D · *Wuoyo* – Talk | Class, home, play, feelings, questions, dialogues | 29 |
| E · *Weche maboyo* – Sentences | Pronouns, one/many, building sentences, first stories | 25 |
| F · *Skul gi piny* – School and world | Numbers to 1000, time, maths English, market, health, stories | 24 |
| G · *Kompyuta* – Computer | Computer and internet words, typing, a dialogue at the internet café | 11 |
| Treasure of words | Every remaining word and phrase | 28 |

Three ways in, chosen when a child's profile is made:
**“Achako”** (new: start by listening, then learn to read), **“Anyalo somo”** (I can read: start with words),
**“I speak English”** (learn Dholuo).

### The reading and writing course (stage B)

1. **Before letters:** tracing lines, slants, zigzags, circles, cups, humps and waves, the movements every letter is made of.
2. **One letter per lesson,** in an order chosen so that real words appear early:
   *a m o t i k u n l e p r b d g w y ch s ny dh th ng' ng j h*. Every letter lesson has these steps:
   - meet the letter: it draws itself stroke by stroke, with its key word and picture (*m · mama*);
   - **first sound:** which picture starts with this letter? (Children know the Dholuo words, so this works without audio);
   - **guided tracing:** numbered start dot, arrows, one stroke at a time. A wrong direction gets a gentle demonstration;
   - **find the letter** among letters that look alike (b d p q, m n u …);
   - tracing on dots only, then **writing freely on the four lines**. The app checks shape and position on the lines;
   - **blending:** consonant + vowel → syllable (*m + a → ma*);
   - **reading real words** made only of letters already learned: read the word and choose the picture,
     build it from syllables or letters, trace a syllable and a short word.
3. **A review every three letters:** reading words, building words, an on-screen keyboard with only the known letters,
   tracing and copying words, and as soon as possible **reading sentences with a true/false picture check**
   (*Ma en diel.* + 🐐 → ✅).
4. **Capital letters** (grouped by how they are written) with names and places: *Achieng', Otieno, Kisumu, Siaya …*
5. **Writing numbers 0–9** with counting.
6. **Reading sentences** for fluency.
7. **32 reading booklets** (*Buge mag somo*), from *mama · ot · kom · it* to short stories. The app checks every word and
   places each booklet right after the review where its last letter was learned, so a child can always read every word.
   Children read each page and choose the matching picture, then read the whole booklet (with English below each line)
   and copy a sentence. Booklets before the capital letters are written in small letters on purpose.

**Mastery, not speed.** In the reading and writing stages a lesson only counts as done with 2 of 3 stars.
With fewer, the child sees “Tem kendo! 💪” and repeats it; after 3 tries the path opens anyway, so nobody gets stuck.
Letters are part of the daily review (🔁) like words: letters a child found hard come back sooner
(find the letter, first sound, write it from memory).

**Reading check (📏).** In Helpers → Learners, a 5-minute check the child does alone, modelled on the
Early Grade Reading Assessment used in Kenyan schools: letter–sound knowledge, word reading and sentence reading,
each counted as correct answers in one minute, plus spelling 4 words. Results are kept per child with the date,
so helpers can see over weeks whether a child is really learning to read.

Which words a lesson uses is computed from the letters a child has learned. Every word a native speaker corrects
or adds becomes reading material automatically.

### English letters and first English reading (stage B2)

Letters in phonics order (*s a t p i n m d g o c k e u r h b f l j v w x y z q*), each written in lower and upper case,
short words to read as soon as their letters are known (*map, pin, dog, cat, sun, bed, box …*),
three sets of sight words (*I, the, is, see, you, can, like, have …*) read in picture sentences, and *sh ch th ee oo*.

### Practising and motivation

Stars per lesson, seeds that grow a plant into a tree, a daily goal of three lessons, day streaks, badges,
spaced repetition of weak words, a memory game, a two-player game, dialogues and stories.

## Content

About 680 words, 200 phrases, 85 sentences, 10 dialogues, 8 stories, 32 reading booklets, 110 syllables, 26 English letters,
40 short English words, 30 sight words, all with pictures.

**Important:** the Dholuo was written without a native speaker. 93 entries are marked as uncertain, and the rest can
also contain mistakes, other dialect forms or unusual spellings. The helper mode exists to fix this.

## For helpers (native speakers)

Open **Helpers / Jokony** on the first screen. See [HELPERS.md](HELPERS.md).
Check and correct the Dholuo, record your voice (start with the 110 syllables: the reading course needs them most),
and add your own words. `review/dholuo-review.csv` lists every entry for checking outside the app.

## Voices

Every word, sentence, letter, name and question the app speaks is a fixed entry that can be **recorded by a person**,
in English as well as in Dholuo (about 1,900 English and 1,600 Dholuo entries).
Recorded voices are shipped with the app and always win. Where nothing is recorded yet, the tablet's own
text-to-speech voice reads English (and the Swahili voice reads Dholuo); both can be switched off in Helpers → Settings.

- **Record:** Helpers → *Record English* / *Record Dholuo*. “In lesson order” starts with what children meet first.
- **Polished automatically:** when the app is built, every recording is trimmed (silence at start and end),
  brought to the same loudness and stored as a small `.m4a` file (needs `ffmpeg`; the deploy workflow installs it).
- One clear, calm voice per language is best: children get used to it. A Kenyan English speaker, for example a teacher,
  gives children the English they will hear at school.

## How helpers' work gets into the app for everyone

```
tablet in the village ──“Export everything”──▶ one file ──WhatsApp / e-mail / upload──▶ contributions/ in this repo
        ▲                                                                                        │
        └──────────── every tablet gets it the next time it opens the app with internet ◀── deploy workflow builds
```

1. On each tablet, helpers enter their name and tick the agreement (CC BY-SA 4.0, names in the thank-you list), then
   tap **Export everything**. The file holds all corrections, own words and recordings of that tablet.
   Tablets can also **Import** each other's files, so one lead tablet can collect everything.
2. Upload the files into `contributions/` (on github.com: open the folder → *Add file* → *Upload files* → *Commit*).
   Any number of files, in any sub-folder, also repeated exports of the same tablet.
3. The deploy workflow runs `build.py`, which runs `tools/merge_contributions.py`:
   corrections and new words go into `src/data_community.js`, recordings into `audio/`, and
   `review/community-report.md` (also shown in the workflow summary) lists who helped and **where helpers disagree**.
   For the same entry the newest version wins.
4. Checked entries lose their “not sure” mark, and recorded words and syllables are played instead of the Swahili voice.
   Helpers appear in the app's thank-you list.

Recordings made outside the app can go to `contributions/audio/luo/<entry-id>.m4a` (or .mp3, .ogg, .webm, .wav).
The entry ids are in `review/dholuo-review.csv`.

## Setting up a tablet

1. Open the app address once in Chrome **with internet**, wait a moment, then Menu ⋮ → “Install app”.
2. Android Settings → System → Languages → Text-to-speech (Speech Services by Google) → install the voice data for
   **English** (Kenya or UK) and **Swahili (Kenya)** while internet is available.
   English words are spoken by this voice. Until real Dholuo recordings exist, the Swahili voice reads Dholuo
   syllables and words (the spelling is very similar). It can be switched off in Helpers → Settings.
3. Allow the microphone when asked. Test: airplane mode on, open the app.

Progress and recordings stay on the tablet. “Clear browser data” deletes them.

## Development

```
src/            the app: content (data_*.js), logic (app_*.js), styles (app.css), handwriting strokes (data_hand.js)
                data_community.js is generated from contributions/ – do not edit it by hand
build.py        merges contributions/ and builds index.html (one self-contained file, font included, not kept in git)
tools/          make_font.py (school font from data_hand.js), merge_contributions.py (helpers' exports → app)
contributions/  what helpers exported from their tablets (the source of all community content)
icons/ manifest.webmanifest sw.js      served files; audio/ is generated
.github/workflows/deploy.yml           on every push to main: merge, build, publish to GitHub Pages
```

- Change content or code in `src/` and push. The deploy workflow builds `index.html` (it is not kept in git).
  To try changes locally: `pip install fonttools`, `python3 build.py`, then open `index.html` in a browser.
- The workflow sets the cache versions in `sw.js` automatically. Tablets pick up a new version the next time
  they open the app with internet.
- Letter shapes: edit `src/data_hand.js`. The font and the tracing both follow automatically.
